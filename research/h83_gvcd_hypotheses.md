## Detailed Specification of Each Hypothesis

### Rank 1 — `H83-1`: Counterfactual Off-Catalogue Structural-Relay & Hollow-Core Tip-Ray Geometry
- **Layers Used**: Published fault catalogue `data/bridge/existing_faults.tif` (restricted strictly to `fold["visible"]` during holdout evaluation and to synthetically re-withheld `syn_state` masks during training).
- **Physical Signature**:
  - Fault networks in the Basin and Range grow by segment linkage across **along-strike continuation zones** ($0.3\text{ km}$–$2.5\text{ km}$ beyond mapped tips), **en-echelon step-over relay ramps** (`ns_gap = sqrt(f * b)` where both forward and backward oriented half-kernels fire simultaneously), and **hanging-wall antithetic sibling faults** (`ns_sib` at $400\text{ m}$–$2.0\text{ km}$ across strike).
  - Crucially, our hollow-core directional operators (`net_tip_ray`, `net_beam_short/med/long`, `net_bridge_med/long`) zero out lags $s \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}\text{ px}$ and integrate strictly over $s \in [4, 40]\text{ px}$, while training uses $N_{\text{syn}} = 2$ counterfactual whole-component withhold draws (`synthetic_withhold`, `buffer_px = 3`).
- **Why It Catches Unmapped Faults**: Aerial-photo and 30 m DEM compilers frequently terminate fault traces where a range-front segment steps across an alluvial embayment or splits into a relay ramp. The bilateral geometric mean $\sqrt{f_\theta(x)\,b_\theta(x)}$ and hollow-core tip ray peak precisely inside the unmapped bridge connecting two mapped tips.
- **Named Non-Fault Mimic & Deconfounding**: Erosional pediment embayments between two unrelated fault segments could create geometric alignments; we deconfound this by requiring multi-physics & geothermal corroboration (`H83-3` to `H83-5`) in the 82-channel cross-tree ensemble.
- **Difference from Prior GEMSDOE Implementations**: Prior lanes either used isotropic Euclidean distance $d(x, C)$ (which collapses to $d=0$ on training positives) or used only 10 `netsib` channels (`GEMSDOE77` `H83-NETSIB`) without hollow-core tip rays, without geothermal/LiDAR coupling, and with an unconstrained Poisson-disk emitter that failed the registry overlap check (`0.9091 > 0.70`).

### Rank 2 — `H83-2`: Collar-Decoupled Quadrant-Calibrated Dinkelbach Submodular $300\text{ m}$ Kernel Optimizer
- **Layers Used**: Continuous 4-fold LOQO probability surface $P_{\text{gvcd}}(x) \in [0, 1]$, `existing_faults.tif` ($d(x, C) > 3.0\text{ px}$ collar), and the 119 prior registry rasters in `data/raw/scored/*.tif`.
- **Physical & Mathematical Signature**:
  - Regional geophysical coverage (especially 1 m 3DEP LiDAR tiles and dense GDR well clusters) varies across the NW, NE, SW, and SE quadrants of the Great Basin. `bayes_prior_calibrate` calibrates each quadrant's log-odds so its mean posterior equals $\pi_{\text{true}} = 0.0022$.
  - `emit_dinkelbach_submodular` then solves the submodular maximum-coverage problem over the 29-cell $300\text{ m}$ triangular kernel $k(d) = \max(1 - d/3, 0)$ using a Minoux lazy-greedy priority queue at collar $d(x, C) > 3.0\text{ px}$ and minimum spacing $d_{\text{sep}} = 2.40\text{ px}$, while simultaneously enforcing `overlap_cap = 0.650 < 0.70` against every discriminating registry raster.
- **Why It Catches Unmapped Faults**: By calibrating per quadrant and accounting for 300 m kernel saturation $M_S(y)$, the optimizer stops over-packing redundant dots in high-relief NW/SW scarps and reallocates budget to genuine unmapped fault continuations and geothermal step-overs across all four quadrants.
- **Difference from Prior GEMSDOE Implementations**: `GEMSDOE32` and `GEMSDOE77` `H83-NETSIB` used uncalibrated greedy Poisson-disk (`min_sep = 2.8 px`) at `excl_px = 2.0`; `GEMSDOE77` `H85-EDTI` used a $3 \times 3$ point-maxima filter and accidentally emitted `201,260` dots (`IR-83-03`). Our `H83-GVCD` Dinkelbach emitter emits the exact `37,654` budget, achieves `0.206817` dual-seed `HOLDOUT-DTI` (`+0.032185` over Poisson-disk on the exact same surface), and satisfies `<= 0.6500 < 0.70` overlap in one unified pass.

### Rank 3 — `H83-3`: Deep Hydrothermal Upflow Plumes, Geothermometer Enthalpy & Clay-Cap Alteration Conduits Coupled to Fault Tips
- **Layers Used**: DOE GDR #1391 `gdr_wellspring_in_footprint.csv` (`27,092` wells/springs: `temp_c`, `gt_quartz_c`, `gt_chalcedony_c`, `gt_na_k_ca_c` — strictly excluding `dist_known_fault_px`), `gdr_volcanic_vents_in_footprint.csv` (`21` Quaternary vents), Band 17 `conductivity_surface`, Band 4 `geodetic_second_invariant`, Band 16 `earthquake_density`.
- **Physical Signature**:
  - In the amagmatic extensional Great Basin, high-temperature geothermal systems ($>35^\circ\text{C}$ measured, $>80^\circ\text{C}$ quartz/cation geothermometer reservoir temperature) occur overwhelmingly at **quaternary fault terminations, horse-tail splays, and dilational step-over relay ramps** where fracture permeability remains critically stressed (Faulds et al., 2011, *INGENIOUS* framework).
  - Hydrothermal alteration forms an electrically conductive smectite/illite clay cap (`z_conductivity_surface > 0`) above the upflow conduit. We construct `geotherm_vent_plume_s6`, `geotherm_vent_plume_s18`, `geotherm_reservoir_enthalpy`, `geotherm_claycap_conduit`, and explicit fault-tip/bridge couplings `coup_geotherm_tip` and `coup_geotherm_gap`.
- **Named Non-Fault Mimic & Deconfounding**: Playas and evaporite salt flats have high shallow electrical conductivity (`conductivity_surface`) without fault conduits, while deep laterally flowing warm aquifers can shift thermal wells down-gradient. Combining silica/cation geothermometer enthalpy + geodetic strain invariant + seismicity + fault-tip stress shadows isolates structural upflow conduits from flat-lying playa brines.
- **Difference from Prior GEMSDOE Implementations**: Neither `GEMSDOE32` nor `GEMSDOE77` (`H83-NETSIB`, `H85-EDTI`, `H77-Step`) rasterized the GDR #1391 chemical geothermometer (`gt_quartz_c`, `gt_chalcedony_c`, `gt_na_k_ca_c`) and thermal spring plumes or coupled them to fault-tip rays and step-over bridges.

### Rank 4 — `H83-4`: Relief-Deconfounded 1 m LiDAR Piedmont Breaklines & GeoDAWN Potassium-Metasomatism ($K/\text{Th}$) + Shallow Magnetic Residual
- **Layers Used**: USGS 3DEP 1 m `lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif` (12 bands), USGS GeoDAWN `geodawn_rad_u8.tif` (`K`, `Th`, `U`, `TC`) and `geodawn_extensions_u8.tif` (`ThK`, `UK`, `UTh`, `TMI_up150`), Band 14 `total_magnetic_intensity`, Band 19 `detrended_elevation_slope`.
- **Physical Signature**:
  - Divides 1 m LiDAR directional scarp step height by local relief (`lidar_relief_norm_scarp`) and weights by 100 m breakline coherence (`lidar_scarp_coherence`) so subtle 0.5–2 m Holocene fault scarps cutting low-relief alluvial fans stand out above noisy bedrock cliff slopes.
  - Computes potassium-over-thorium metasomatic enrichment (`rad_k_metasomatism = z(K) - 0.75 z(Th)`) and shallow magnetic vertical-wavelength residual (`mag_shallow_residual = z(TMI) - z(TMI_up150)`), plus strike-continuation coupling `coup_lidar_cont`.
- **Named Non-Fault Mimic & Deconfounding**: Pleistocene Lake Lahontan wave-cut shorelines and fluvial terrace risers produce low-relief 1 m LiDAR steps (`lidar_step_max`), while potassic felsic tuffs elevate raw `K`. Normalizing by Th (`K/Th`), requiring subsurface magnetic/gravity gradient alignment, and coupling with mapped fault strike continuation (`coup_lidar_cont`) separates tectonic scarps from lacustrine strandlines.

### Rank 5 — `H83-5`: Basement-Step Gravity Horizontal-Gradient Curvature & Transtensional Dilatation-Shear Tensor Coupling
- **Layers Used**: Band 7 `geodetic_shear_rate`, Band 8 `geodetic_dilatation_rate`, Band 13 `iso_gravity_anomaly`, Band 15 `depth_to_basement`, Band 18 `iso_gravity_horizontal_gradient`.
- **Physical Signature**:
  - Buried intrabasin normal and transtensional faults offset dense Paleozoic carbonate/crystalline basement against low-density Cenozoic basin fill, creating linear ridges in horizontal gravity gradient (`grad_iso_gravity_anomaly`) and basement-depth slope (`grad_depth_to_basement`).
  - Where those buried basement steps coincide with active GPS transtensional strain (`x_strain_transtension = z(shear) + 0.5 z(dilatation)`) and parallel sibling fault geometry (`coup_strain_sib`), blind intrabasin faults are recovered even under thick Playas or Quaternary valley fill.
- **Named Non-Fault Mimic & Deconfounding**: Pluton/caldera margins produce curved gravity gradients without active transtensional strain or linear strike coherence; coupling with geodetic shear/dilatation and directional fault-network geometry rejects circular intrusive contacts.
