Did the GEMSDOE32 B=2 file score 0.2778? — PhD answer (corrected 2026-10-21)

The competition metric is a budget: DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw) with a 300 m (3 px) triangular kernel. For binary emission, denominator grows by exactly α=0.2 per emitted pixel regardless of location ( dD = α·(k + 1−k) = α ). Numerator grows by at most kernel weight k≤1. So marginal DTI >0 iff k > α·DTI ≈0.054 at DTI 0.27 (derived in src/gems83/metric.py, verified by brute force).

GEMSDOE32’s h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros.tif (37,654 dots, 0.2778 owner-claimed, not organizer-verified) beat the 0.2708 base by deleting every dot within 2 px (200 m) of the published catalogue. Why B=2 plausibly helped (conditional, not proven cause of the claimed 0.2778): officials mask pixels exactly on known faults in both rounds (forum 11516, DrivenData staff), so on-catalogue dots cost nothing; gain can only come from unmasked 1–2 px flank dots whose marginal kernel credit k falls below the bar α·DTI≈0.054. GEMSDOE32's own site labels this file UNSCORED (projected 0.2747 MODEL) — the 0.2778 figure is an owner claim with no organizer receipt, verified 2026-10-21. Live-anchored inversion recovers TPw=5,073, D=18,734, |G|≈12,226, recall 0.415, break-even τ=0.05416; B=2 removal spends 65 credits for 2,545 dots, budget 135.7 → safety 2.08, projected live 0.2747 (MODEL, not score). Two routes (empirical 0.0548 vs derived 0.0520) agree. Can we beat 0.2778? Only by raising mean credit per dot ~25% at same mass — no public catalogue supplies that (GDR QFaults v2: 59,065 px, all but 1 within 300 m of catalogue).
