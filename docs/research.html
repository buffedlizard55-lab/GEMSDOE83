What the H33-2 operation actually does

The owner-authored GEMSDOE32 implementation calculates distance from each cell to the mapped catalogue and deletes base predictions within a chosen buffer. H33-2-B2 starts from an owner-reported 0.2708 base claim (40,199 dots; USER-REPORTED, NOT ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED, with no receipt tied to the base artifact), removes 2,545 dots at distance ≤2 pixels, and leaves 37,654 dots. On that 100 m grid, the B=2 cutoff is 200 m. This is catalogue-aware pruning of an existing pattern, not a standalone geological detector.

The inspected owner source says the organizer masks existing-fault pixels during scoring and argues that dots spent on that masked area add prediction penalty without gaining credit there. That rule statement is from owner code, not freshly verified against the official problem/scorer in this session. Conditional on the rule, removing low-expected-credit dots can raise the precision-weighted score while preserving coverage of unseen targets. It is therefore plausible that H33-2-B2 performed well through prediction-mass reallocation, not because it independently mapped new fault geology.

What can—and cannot—be concluded

  • Plausible: the B=2 buffer removed a relatively small fraction of the incumbent dot mass, and the owner’s own proxy favored this moderate prune over the unpruned base and the more aggressive B=3 variant.
  • Not demonstrated: that the reported 0.2778 belongs to the exact TIFF hash above, that the score change was caused by H33-2 alone, or that the chosen dots correspond to geological faults.
  • Not transferable as a result: a custom proxy or projected live value is not the required canonical `HOLDOUT-DTI`, and it is not an organizer score. No current GEMSDOE83 holdout was run.
  • Leaderboard warning: the user supplied conflicting current maxima, 0.3195 and 0.4413. The latter rounds from an owner-local proxy value in a different scan. Neither claim was fetched from the current official leaderboard.
